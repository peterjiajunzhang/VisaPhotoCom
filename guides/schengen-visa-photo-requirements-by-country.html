Schengen visa photo requirements: the 35 × 45 mm baseline and where countries differ

By the Visa Photo 1-Tap developer · Official sources checked November 14, 2026

The European Commission asks for one thing: a photo that complies with ICAO standards. Individual Schengen countries — and sometimes individual embassies of the same country — then add their own details, such as how recent the photo must be or which background shades they accept. This guide gives the shared baseline and a comparison table that only includes what we could confirm on an official page today.

What is the short answer?

Bring a recent colour photo, 35 mm wide and 45 mm high, taken to ICAO standards: full face looking straight at the camera, neutral expression, eyes open, on a plain light background. Then read the checklist of the specific embassy or consulate you apply to, because photo age (3 or 6 months), background shade and glasses rules differ between countries and even between missions of the same country.

Which differences matter most?

Photo age: 3 months or 6 months

The German Embassy in London asks for photos no older than three months, while the German Embassy in Kuala Lumpur and Denmark’s visa page say six months. If you are unsure, use a photo taken within the last three months; it satisfies both.

Background shade

Most pages say “light” without naming a colour. The German Embassy in London specifies white or off-white. Dutch passport rules allow light grey, light blue or white, and Greek passport rules prefer a specific grey — but those two pages are not visa rules. A plain white or very light background is accepted by every visa page in the table that names a shade.

Face size

Bulgaria and the German Embassy in London ask for the face to fill 70–80% of the photo (32–36 mm of the 45 mm height). The Czech page measures eyes-to-chin instead (at least 13 mm). In practice, a tightly framed head-and-shoulders photo satisfies both.

Glasses

Rules range from “no sunglasses” (Czech Republic) to “glasses allowed if the frames do not cover the eyes” (German Embassy London). None of the visa pages in the table requires glasses to be worn, so removing them is the simplest way to avoid glare and frame problems.

What are the most common Schengen photo mistakes?

  • Using a 2 × 2 inch (51 × 51 mm) US-size photo. The Schengen size is 35 × 45 mm, a taller rectangle.
  • A photo older than the mission allows — three months at some posts.
  • A head that is too small in the frame, often from cropping a wide holiday photo.
  • Shadows on the face or background, or a textured wall.
  • Tinted glasses, glare on lenses, or hair covering the eyes.
  • Printing on ordinary paper instead of photo-quality paper.

Whose rules apply to me?

Apply to the consulate of the country that is your main destination, and follow that consulate’s checklist. Visa service providers such as VFS Global, TLScontact or BLS International often publish checklists for the consulates they serve; they are useful, but the consulate’s own rules decide. Many consulates also scan your photo or take one when you give fingerprints, so what you bring is not always the image that ends up in the visa.

Frequently asked questions

What size is a Schengen visa photo?

35 mm wide by 45 mm high, in colour and to ICAO standards. Every national visa page in our table that gives a size uses 35 × 45 mm.

Does a Schengen visa photo need a white background?

Most official pages say a light or light-coloured background. The German Embassy in London asks for white or off-white. A plain white or very light background meets every visa page we checked.

How recent must a Schengen visa photo be?

It depends on the mission: the German Embassy in London says no older than three months, while Denmark and the German Embassy in Kuala Lumpur say six months. A photo from the last three months satisfies both.

Can I wear glasses in a Schengen visa photo?

Some missions allow glasses if the frames do not cover the eyes; sunglasses are not accepted. Removing glasses avoids glare and frame problems.
