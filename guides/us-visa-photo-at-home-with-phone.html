How to take a 2x2 US visa photo at home with a phone

By the Visa Photo 1-Tap developer · Official sources checked November 14, 2026

You are allowed to take your own US visa photo, but the Department of State is blunt about phones: it says most webcams and mobile phones cannot provide images of sufficient quality. A good home photo is therefore about removing every quality problem you can control — light, distance, background, pose and sharpness — before you think about cropping.

What is the short answer?

Stand in front of a plain white or off-white wall, facing a window with soft daylight. Have someone hold the phone at your eye level at least 4 feet (1.2 m) away, using the main rear camera with no filters or portrait mode. Look straight at the camera with a neutral expression, both eyes open and no glasses. Leave space around your head and shoulders, then crop to a square where your head is 50%–69% of the height.

What do I need to take a visa photo at home?

  • A phone with a clean rear camera lens.
  • A helper, or a tripod or stable surface with the self-timer.
  • A plain white or off-white wall. If you do not have one, the Department of State suggests covering a non-white wall with a white blanket or sheet; pull it flat so it has no folds.
  • A window with indirect daylight, or two similar lamps you can place either side of the camera.

How should I light the photo?

The official examples reject photos that are underexposed, photos where the colour is inaccurate, and photos with shadows on the face or background. In practice that means soft, even light from the front:

  • Face a window with indirect daylight. Light from one side brightens one cheek and leaves the other in shadow.
  • Avoid a single ceiling light straight above you: it puts shadows in the eye sockets and under the nose and chin.
  • Avoid direct flash. If red-eye appears, retake the photo; the Department of State allows the camera’s red-eye reduction while shooting, but not removing red-eye with editing software afterwards.
  • Mixed light (a warm lamp plus blue daylight) can tint skin and the background. If the colour looks wrong, turn off the lamp or change the camera’s white balance, as the official examples suggest.

How far away should the camera be?

The Department of State’s examples say the camera should be at least four feet (1.2 m) from the person. At arm’s length, a phone’s front camera exaggerates the nose and shrinks the ears, which is why a selfie rarely looks like a passport-style portrait. Use the main rear camera at 1× (or 2× if your phone has it), hold it at eye level, keep it level rather than tilted up or down, and tap on your face to focus. Take a burst of shots and keep the sharpest one.

What background works best?

Plain white or off-white, with no texture, objects or lines and without shadows. Stand a step or two away from the wall rather than leaning against it, so your own shadow falls low and soft behind you. Keep door frames, switches, pictures and plants out of the frame. A background you get right in the original photo does not need to be changed later.

How should I pose and what should I wear?

  • Face the camera directly with your shoulders square. Do not tilt your head to the side, forward or back.
  • Keep a neutral expression with both eyes open. Unusual expressions and squinting are not accepted.
  • Remove your glasses, and make sure they are not resting on your head. They are allowed only in rare medical cases with a signed statement.
  • Keep hair away from your face and eyes.
  • Wear everyday clothing. Uniforms and camouflage are not allowed, except religious attire worn daily. Remove hats, headphones and wireless earbuds; religious head coverings worn daily are allowed if they do not cover your face or cast shadows on it.
  • Frame from mid-chest to well above your head. You need room to crop a square with your head at 50%–69% of the height.

What are the most common mistakes?

  • Arm’s-length selfies that distort the face.
  • Portrait mode, beauty filters or skin smoothing. The photo must not be digitally enhanced or altered to change your appearance.
  • Uneven light, shadows behind the head, or a yellow cast from indoor bulbs.
  • Glasses, earbuds or a hat left on.
  • Framing too tight to crop correctly, or the head too small because the crop is too loose.
  • Saving the result as a screenshot instead of exporting the photo file.
  • Using a photo older than six months, or copying a photo from a driver’s licence or another document, which is not acceptable.

How do I turn the photo into a 2x2 file or print?

For the DS-160, crop the photo to a square with your head at 50%–69% of the height and your eyes at 56%–69% from the bottom, resize to 600–1200 pixels, and export a JPEG of 240 kB or less. The DS-160 upload guide covers HEIC conversion and compression. For a printed 2 × 2 inch photo, print the square at 2 × 2 inches on matte or glossy photo-quality paper; a 600 × 600 pixel image printed at that size is 300 pixels per inch, the resolution the Department of State specifies for scanning an existing photo.

Frequently asked questions

Can I take my US visa photo myself with a phone?

Yes. The Department of State recommends a professional photo service but says you may take the photo yourself. It also warns that most webcams and mobile phones cannot provide images of sufficient quality, so use good light, the rear camera and a helper or tripod.

Can I smile in a US visa photo?

Keep a neutral expression with both eyes open. The official examples list a non-neutral expression as unacceptable.

Can I use a selfie?

An arm’s-length selfie is too close. The official examples say the camera should be at least four feet (1.2 m) away, so ask someone to take the photo or use a self-timer.

Does the photo have to be printed?

For the DS-160 you upload a digital image. A printed 2 × 2 inch photo is needed if the upload failed, for immigrant visa interviews, and at posts that ask applicants to bring one, so check your embassy or consulate’s instructions.
