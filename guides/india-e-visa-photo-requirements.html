India e-Visa photo requirements and upload errors: how to fix them

By the Visa Photo 1-Tap developer · Official sources checked November 14, 2026

The Indian e-Visa form asks for two different uploads that people often mix up: a photograph of your face as a JPEG, and a scan of your passport’s bio page as a PDF. Each has its own format and size limits. Most failed uploads come from sending the wrong file to the wrong field or from a file outside those limits.

What is the short answer?

Upload a recent, square JPEG photo of your face between 10 KB and 1 MB, front view with eyes open and no glasses, your full head centred, on a plain light-coloured or white background with no shadows and no border. Upload your passport bio page separately as a PDF between 10 KB and 300 KB. Apply only on the official site, indianvisaonline.gov.in.

Why are the photo and the passport page different uploads?

The e-Visa page lists “Scanned Bio Page of the passport showing the Photograph and Details” as a required document for every e-Visa type, and lists the digital photograph separately. They are checked for different things: the photo shows what you look like now; the bio page shows your identity data. Three mix-ups come up again and again:

  • Uploading the passport scan as the photo. A scan of the whole page is not a square face photo, and the photo field takes JPEG, not PDF.
  • Cropping the face out of the passport page. It is technically a picture of you, but it is not a recent photograph, it carries the passport’s security printing, and the resolution is usually poor. The FAQ asks for a recent photograph; take a new one.
  • Uploading a phone photo of the passport as JPEG. The bio-page field expects a PDF. Scan or photograph the page, then save or export it as a PDF under 300 KB, with all text readable.

What are the common upload errors and how do I fix each?

We could not find a public list of the form’s exact error messages, so the headings below describe the problem rather than quote the screen.

The photo file is larger than 1 MB

A modern phone photo is often 2–5 MB. Crop to a square around your head and shoulders first, then export a JPEG at a smaller pixel size (around 600–1000 pixels per side is plenty for a face photo) and a high quality setting. This usually brings the file well under 1 MB without visible loss.

The photo file is smaller than 10 KB

This happens with thumbnails, images saved from messaging apps, or very aggressive compression. Go back to the original photo and export again at a larger size or higher quality.

The photo is not square

The page requires equal height and width. Crop the original to a square, keeping your whole head and some space around it, and do not stretch the image to fit.

The photo is not a JPEG

iPhones save HEIC by default and screenshots are PNG. Renaming the file does not convert it. Export as JPEG, or set Settings › Camera › Formats › Most Compatible before taking a new photo.

The passport PDF is larger than 300 KB

Scan at a moderate resolution, crop to the bio page only, and save one page. If your scanner app offers a “smaller file” or quality option, use it, then open the PDF and check that every line of text and the machine-readable zone are still sharp.

The page will not accept the file at all

Confirm you selected the right field, the right file and the right format. A technical problem such as a session timeout is different from a rejected file; save your progress and try again later rather than re-editing a file that already meets the limits.

Which photo problems lead to rejection after upload?

The FAQ warns that an application is liable to be rejected if the uploaded documents and photograph are not clear or not as per specification. A file can pass the size check and still be rejected for content, so check the photo itself:

  • Glasses on — the rule says without spectacles.
  • Shadows on the face or behind the head, or a background that is patterned, dark or coloured.
  • Head cut off at the top or chin, or not centred.
  • Looking away, eyes closed, or the face turned to one side.
  • A border around the photo, or a photo of a printed photo.
  • Blur or noise from low light. Stand facing a window in daylight and keep the camera steady.

The guide to taking a photo at home with a phone covers light, distance and background in detail; the same technique works for an Indian e-Visa photo.

How do I check my files before uploading?

  • iPhone: open the photo in Photos and swipe up to see the format, pixel size and file size. In Files, long-press a PDF and choose Get Info to see its size.
  • Mac: in Finder, choose File › Get Info; in Preview, Tools › Show Inspector shows pixel dimensions.
  • Windows: right-click the file, choose Properties, then Details.

Before you pay the fee, review the whole application. The FAQ says applicants are responsible for checking all details, including the photograph, before final submission.

Frequently asked questions

What size should an Indian e-Visa photo be?

The official page asks for a square JPEG between 10 KB and 1 MB. It does not give a pixel size; a square of several hundred pixels per side with your full head centred works within those limits.

Can the background be light grey instead of white?

The requirements allow a plain light-coloured or white background, while the FAQ asks for a white background. White meets both statements.

Can I wear glasses in an Indian e-Visa photo?

No. The requirements say the photo should be taken without spectacles.

Can I use the photo from my passport?

Cropping it from the passport scan is a poor idea: it is not recent, it shows security printing and the quality is usually low. The FAQ asks for a recent front-facing photograph, so take a new one.
