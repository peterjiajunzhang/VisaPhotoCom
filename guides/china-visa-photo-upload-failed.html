CHINA VISA · ONLINE APPLICATION

China visa photo not passing the online upload? Causes and fixes

By the Visa Photo 1-Tap developer · Official sources checked November 14, 2026

The China Online Visa Application system checks your photo automatically as you upload it. When it refuses a photo, the cause is almost always one of a few measurable things: pixel size, file size, format, head and eye position, or background and lighting. This guide explains each one and what to do if the photo still will not pass.

What is the short answer?

Upload a recent colour JPEG between 354 × 472 and 420 × 560 pixels, usually 40–120 KB, on a white or near-white background, with your face straight to the camera and your eyes in the upper part of the frame (at 354 × 472, at least 256 pixels above the bottom edge). If the system still rejects it after several attempts, the Chinese Embassy in the US says you can keep filling in the rest of the form and bring a standard ID photo when you submit your passport.

What are the official digital photo requirements?

The specification below comes from the photo requirements published by Chinese diplomatic missions. It is a single image with Chinese text, dated 2016 and re-posted by the Chinese Embassy in the UK, which the Chinese Visa Application Service Center in London links to from its 2026 notice on uploading materials.

ItemDigital photo (online form)
Pixel sizeAt least 354 × 472, at most 420 × 560 (width × height). Both are a 3:4 portrait shape.
Format and colourJPEG compression, 24-bit RGB colour.
File sizeGenerally 40–120 KB.
Eye positionAt 354 × 472: eyes at least 256 px above the bottom edge.
Top of headAt 354 × 472: hairline 10–70 px below the top edge; the eye rule takes priority if both cannot be met.
Face widthAt 354 × 472: 205 px ± 14 px in the text; the diagram shows 191–251 px.
BackgroundWhite or close to white, no border.
RecencyTaken within the last 6 months.

Source: Chinese Embassy in the UK, “中国签证申请相片规格要求”. The face-width figures in the text and diagram do not agree; aim for the text value (191–219 px at 354 × 472). The same document gives the printed photo size as 33 × 48 mm.

What does the online system check?

The Chinese Embassy in the United States launched the China Online Visa Application system in September 2025; the form is now reached through consular.mfa.gov.cn/VISA/. Its guidance says that when you upload your photo, the system lets you adjust it with dragging and cropping tools and automatically determines whether it meets the requirements. The New York consulate publishes the same wording.

Neither page lists the exact tests the system runs, so nobody outside the system can tell you precisely why a particular photo failed. What you can control is the published specification: pixel dimensions, file size, format, how your head sits in the frame, and the quality of the original photo. Work through those in order, and change one thing at a time so you can tell which change made the difference.

Keep two kinds of failure apart. A photo that the checker rejects is a photo problem. A page that freezes, times out or will not open the file picker is a technical problem; try again later or in another up-to-date browser before you start re-editing a photo that may be fine.

Why does my photo keep failing, and how do I fix it?

1. The pixel size is outside 354 × 472 to 420 × 560

A phone photo is thousands of pixels wide, and a scan of a 33 × 48 mm print is often a different size again. Crop the photo to a 3:4 portrait shape around your head and shoulders, then resize it to a size inside the range. 354 × 472 matches the official example exactly; 420 × 560 is the largest allowed and keeps more detail. Do not stretch a photo to fit: crop first, then resize with the proportions locked.

2. The file is too large or too small

Above about 120 KB, export the JPEG again at a slightly lower quality setting. Below 40 KB, the photo has usually been compressed too hard and may look blocky; export again from the original at a higher quality, or use a larger pixel size within the range. Each re-save of a JPEG loses detail, so always go back to the original rather than compressing an already compressed copy.

3. The file is not really a JPEG

iPhones save photos as HEIC by default, and screenshots are PNG. Renaming photo.heic to photo.jpg does not change the format. Export or save the photo as JPEG in an editor, or change the iPhone camera format to “Most Compatible” (Settings › Camera › Formats) before taking a new photo.

4. Your head is too high, too low or the wrong size

This is the most common cause when the file itself is correct. On a 354 × 472 photo, your eyes must be at least 256 pixels above the bottom edge, which is about 54% of the height. The top of your hair should be 10 to 70 pixels below the top edge, and your face about 191 to 219 pixels wide. If you are using 420 × 560, scale these by the same factor of about 1.19: by our arithmetic, eyes at least about 304 px from the bottom, hair about 12 to 83 px from the top, face about 227 to 260 px wide. These scaled figures are our calculation, not an official table. If the frame is too tight, move back and retake the photo rather than enlarging a crop.

5. The background is not white enough, or there are shadows

The requirement is a white or near-white background without a border. A cream wall in warm indoor light can read as yellow, and a shadow behind your head can make the background uneven. Stand a little away from a plain light wall, face a window or a soft light, and avoid a single ceiling light directly overhead.

6. The face or pose does not meet the requirements

The specification asks for a face looking straight at the camera with complete facial features, both eyes open, mouth closed, ears showing and a natural expression. Your head may turn no more than 20 degrees left or right and tilt no more than 25 degrees up or down. Thick-framed glasses and tinted glasses are not allowed. Hats and head scarves are not recommended; if you wear one for religious reasons, it must not cover any part of your face. The photo must not be too dark or too bright, must show natural skin tone, must have no red-eye, and your eyes should be more than 60 pixels apart.

7. The photo is old, edited or not of you

The photo must have been taken within the last six months. Beauty filters, smoothing and heavy edits can change how your face looks and are a poor idea for any identity photo. The photo must match the applicant whose passport is on the form.

How can I check a photo before uploading it?

  • On an iPhone: open the photo in Photos and swipe up. The information panel shows the format, pixel dimensions and file size.
  • On a Mac: open the file in Preview and choose Tools › Show Inspector, or Tools › Adjust Size to see and change the pixel size.
  • On Windows: right-click the file, choose Properties, then open the Details tab to see dimensions and size.

Then look at the photo itself at full size: is it sharp, is the light even across your face, is the background plain, and do the eyes sit above the middle of the frame? A photo that passes the measurements but looks blurred or dark can still be refused later in the review.

What should I do if the photo still fails?

The Chinese Embassy in the United States says that if your photo does not pass the check after multiple uploads, you may still continue filling out the other sections, and you should bring a standard ID photo when you submit your passport to the Embassy or Consulate-General. The New York consulate gives the same instruction. Procedures are set by the mission or visa centre handling your application, so check its current instructions if you apply outside the United States.

For a printed photo, the same specification gives 33 mm wide × 48 mm high, head width 15–22 mm, head height (chin to crown) 28–33 mm, 3–5 mm from the top of the head to the top edge, and at least 7 mm below the chin. Passing the online check is not the end of the process: the embassy notes that online review approval does not guarantee visa issuance.

Frequently asked questions

Is the 33 × 48 mm photo the same as the digital photo?

No. 33 × 48 mm is the printed photo size. The online form needs a JPEG between 354 × 472 and 420 × 560 pixels. Both use the same portrait shape, so a good digital file can usually be printed at 33 × 48 mm.

Can I upload my square US visa photo?

Not as it is. A US visa photo is square, while the Chinese digital photo is a 3:4 portrait with different head and eye positions. Crop again from an original photo with enough space around your head, or take a new photo.

Can I wear glasses in a China visa photo?

The specification excludes thick-framed glasses and tinted glasses. Thin frames are not explicitly banned, but reflections can hide your eyes, so removing glasses is the simplest choice.

Does passing the online photo check mean the photo is accepted?

No. The online check is one step. Consular staff review the application, and the embassy states that online review approval does not guarantee visa issuance.
